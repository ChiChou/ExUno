## Language-Learning-decks (Italian, Spanish, French)

- © 2025 GENERAL NEURO, https://github.com/vbvss199/Language-Learning-decks. MIT; the notice is in `LICENSES/Language-Learning-decks-MIT.txt`.
- The decks' word-frequency data comes from **wordfreq** (CC BY-SA 4.0), https://github.com/rspeer/wordfreq. Cite: Robyn Speer (2022), *rspeer/wordfreq: v3.0 (v3.0.2)*, Zenodo, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7199437. wordfreq credits SUBTLEX, Wikipedia, OpenSubtitles and other corpora among its sources.
- Translations and example sentences are machine-generated by the deck authors; a few are generic or imperfect.
- Changes in Ex Uno:
  - Entries flagged as not useful for flashcards, or with no CEFR level, are dropped.
  - Semicolons in translations become commas, and POS labels are normalized.
  - Gender and articles: French from the deck's `article_with_word` (for *l'*, from *un/une* in the example). Spanish and Italian only when the deck's own example shows a determiner before the noun.
  - Topic tags are added (below).

## Dickinson College Commentaries Latin Core Vocabulary

- The ~1,000 most frequent Latin lemmata, with principal parts, part of speech, semantic group and frequency rank. Compiled by Christopher Francese and collaborators, Dickinson College Commentaries, https://dcc.dickinson.edu/latin-vocabulary-list.
- License: Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike, per the site's terms (https://dcc.dickinson.edu/terms-use). The terms give no version; BY-SA lets adaptations use a later version, so the pack is CC BY-SA 4.0. The site is "intended for free educational use"; DCC asks to hear how the material is used (dickinsoncommentaries@gmail.com).
- Downloaded from the CSV copy at https://github.com/sjhuskey/latin-vocabulary (`dcc-latin-core-list.csv`). The data and its license are DCC's.
- Changes in Ex Uno:
  - Headwords are split into lemma and principal parts, and gender is read from the headword.
  - Frequency ranks are grouped into four levels (I: 1–250, II: 251–500, III: 501–750, IV: 751–1000).
  - DCC's semantic groups are regrouped into 13 topics with Latin names.

## Tatoeba (grammar course sentences)

- Sentences and English translations by the contributors of Tatoeba, https://tatoeba.org. License: CC BY 2.0 FR (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/fr/); the legal code is in `LICENSES/CC-BY-2.0-FR.txt`. Terms of use: https://tatoeba.org/en/terms_of_use.
- Downloaded from OPUS's dated release of the corpus, `OPUS-Tatoeba/v2026-07-08` (Moses format), https://opus.nlpl.eu/Tatoeba/corpus/version/Tatoeba. Cite: J. Tiedemann, 2012, *Parallel Data, Tools and Interfaces in OPUS*, Proceedings of LREC 2012.
- The OPUS text release has no per-sentence ids or authors, so contributors are credited as a group. Any sentence can be found on tatoeba.org by searching its text.
- Changes in Ex Uno: one word or short phrase per sentence is replaced by a gap, and other forms of the same word are added as wrong options. Translations are kept as published; when a sentence had several, the shortest is used.

## UniMorph (grammar course paradigms)

- Inflection tables for Italian, Spanish, French and Latin, extracted from English Wiktionary by the UniMorph project, https://unimorph.github.io and https://github.com/unimorph. License: CC BY-SA 3.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/). Wiktionary text is by the Wiktionary contributors.
- Cite: A. D. McCarthy et al., *UniMorph 3.0: Universal Morphology*, Proceedings of LREC 2020; K. Batsuren et al., *UniMorph 4.0: Universal Morphology*, Proceedings of LREC 2022.
- Use in Ex Uno: the forms that compete in a gap (for example the six present forms of a verb, or the cases of a Latin noun). Latin verb forms are not taken from UniMorph; they come from Ex Uno's own rules with DCC principal parts.

## Online services (called at runtime, nothing bundled)

- **Wiktionary REST API** (`en.wiktionary.org/api/rest_v1/page/definition/…`): definitions are shown with a link back. Text is CC BY-SA 4.0 by the Wiktionary contributors. Requests send a User-Agent with the project URL, as Wikimedia's API etiquette asks.
- **Frankfurter** (https://frankfurter.dev): European Central Bank reference rates; shown as approximations.
- **Apple speech synthesis**: the voices installed on the device. Latin is read by an Italian voice.
